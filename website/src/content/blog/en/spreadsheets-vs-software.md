Every Malawian business starts its books in a spreadsheet. It is flexible,
free and familiar. But there comes a point where the costs of that flexibility
outweigh the benefits.

## The five signs you've outgrown Excel

1. **You re-key the same numbers twice.** Sales go in one sheet, invoices in
   another, and they never quite match.
2. **A formula breaks and nobody notices.** One wrong cell reference can
   silently corrupt a year of records.
3. **Your accountant asks for reports you can't produce.** A profit & loss
   statement is hard when income lives in three tabs.
4. **The file lives on one person's laptop.** If they're away — or the laptop
   dies — the books are gone.
5. **You're doing tax by hand.** Computing PAYE and VAT in formulas is
   error-prone and stressful.

## What changes with real software

- **One entry, everywhere.** Record an invoice once; it updates income,
  stock and reports.
- **Integrity you can trust.** Double-entry journals that check themselves.
- **Multi-user, with roles.** Your bookkeeper and accountant work in the same
  books without stepping on each other.
- **MRA-ready reports** at filing time.

## When to switch

If you hit *any* of the five signs above, the switch will pay for itself —
usually in the first filing season. Start with the free plan, import your
records, and run both in parallel for a month.

Spreadsheets were never the problem — doing accounting *in* them is.
